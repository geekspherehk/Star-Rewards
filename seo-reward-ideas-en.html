Your reward shelf makes or breaks the whole points system. The rule is simple: experiences > stuff, cheap-but-special > expensive. Here's a copy-ready list by age.

1. Three Rules for a Great Reward Shelf

  • Experiences first: a one-on-one game or staying up 15 minutes late beats a new toy — and costs nothing.
  • Tier it: small (same-day), medium (within a week), big (monthly goal) — so there's always something within reach.
  • Keep costs bounded: "pick the family's weekend destination" is nearly free but carries real weight.

7. FAQ

Should rewards cost money?
Most shouldn't. Privileges and experiences (later bedtime, choosing dinner) are free, and kids value them more than toys.
What if my child asks for a huge expensive reward?
Put it on the shelf as a long-term goal with a high price, and let saving do the teaching. Don't buy it "just this once."
How many rewards should the shelf have?
6–12 items across three tiers. Too few and there's nothing to want; too many and focus dissolves.
